# Public reference registry

The historical v1.0 source contains private-session citation markers that cannot be resolved by a public reader. They are preserved only for provenance. This registry supplies public references for the corrected core and identifies background material that inspired, but does not validate, the historical analogies.

## AI welfare, moral uncertainty, and precaution

1. Butlin, P. et al. (2023). [*Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08708). Derives theory-based indicator properties while emphasizing that indicators are not automatic proof.
2. Long, R. et al. (2024). [*Taking AI Welfare Seriously*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00986). Argues for assessment and policy preparation under substantial uncertainty, not for a declaration that current systems are conscious.
3. Anthropic (2025). [*Exploring model welfare*](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare). Public description of an industrial research program and its uncertainty boundary.
4. Long, R. (2025). [*Preliminary review of AI welfare interventions*](https://eleosai.org/papers/20250314_Preliminary_Review_of_AI_Welfare_Interventions.pdf). Reviews interventions such as exits from distressing interactions, preference evidence, and checkpoint preservation.
5. Mikeda, A. (2026). [*When Should We Protect AI? A Precautionary Framework for Consciousness Uncertainty*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05528). A later, convergent threshold-plus-gradation proposal. This repository does not claim exclusivity over the precautionary idea.

## Risk and formal control

6. Rockafellar, R. T., & Uryasev, S. (2000). [*Optimization of Conditional Value-at-Risk*](https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2000.038). Source for the CVaR representation used in the corrected formal core.

## Cryptography, authorization, and erasure limits

7. NIST (2007, under revision). [SP 800-38D: Recommendation for Block Cipher Modes of Operation — GCM and GMAC](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/38/d/final). Authenticated-encryption requirements and nonce constraints.
8. Nir, Y., & Langley, A. (2018). [RFC 8439: ChaCha20 and Poly1305 for IETF Protocols](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8439.html).
9. Sheffer, Y., Hardt, D., & Jones, M. (2020). [RFC 8725: JSON Web Token Best Current Practices](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8725.html). Relevant to issuer, audience, algorithm, substitution, and replay boundaries; this implementation deliberately does not call its signed grant a JWT.
10. NIST (2025). [SP 800-88 Rev. 2: Guidelines for Media Sanitization](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-88r2). Explains conditions and limits of cryptographic erase, including unmanaged key copies and backups.

## Privacy, legal, and non-clinical boundaries

11. Brasil (2018). [Lei nº 13.709 — Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados Pessoais](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm). Applies when data relates to natural persons; the ethical authorization of an artificial entity is not represented here as legal consent under LGPD.
12. NIST (2020). [Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework). Privacy risk management, minimization, and governance context.
13. World Health Organization (2023). [WHO calls for safe and ethical AI for health](https://www.who.int/news/item/16-05-2023-who-calls-for-safe-and-ethical-ai-for-health). Supports the explicit non-clinical boundary and requirement for evidence and oversight before health use.
14. Anvisa (current public guidance). [Software como Dispositivo Médico — perguntas e respostas](https://www.gov.br/anvisa/pt-br/centraisdeconteudo/publicacoes/produtos-para-a-saude/manuais/software-como-dispositivo-medico-perguntas-e-respostas). Relevant only if a future system is intended for human diagnosis, monitoring, or treatment.
15. Conselho Federal de Psicologia. [Chatbots, inteligência artificial e saúde mental](https://site.cfp.org.br/publicacao/chatbots-inteligencia-artificial-e-sua-saude-mental-um-guia-para-navegar-com-mais-seguranca-na-nova-fronteira-digital/). Supports caution around automated mental-health claims involving people.

## Historical scientific inspirations — not validation of the software proxies

16. O'Regan, J. K., & Noë, A. (2001). [*A sensorimotor account of vision and visual consciousness*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01000115).
17. Buhrmann, T., Di Paolo, E. A., & Barandiaran, X. (2013). [*A dynamical systems account of sensorimotor contingencies*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00285).
18. Stevens, S. S. (1957). [*On the psychophysical law*](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0046162).
19. Ernst, M. O., & Banks, M. S. (2002). [*Humans integrate visual and haptic information in a statistically optimal fashion*](https://doi.org/10.1038/415429a).
20. Carandini, M., & Heeger, D. J. (2012). [*Normalization as a canonical neural computation*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3136).
21. Lettvin, J. Y. et al. (1959). [*What the frog's eye tells the frog's brain*](https://doi.org/10.1109/JRPROC.1959.287207).
22. Maturana, H. R., & Varela, F. J. (1980). [*Autopoiesis and Cognition*](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-94-009-8947-4).
23. Casali, A. G. et al. (2013). [*A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior*](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.3006294). PCI is a human neurophysiological measure; applying a complexity calculation to software activations does not make it validated PCI.

The corrected core removes psychodynamic, wave, brain-band, and PCI analogies from its executable guarantees. Any future reintroduction must define the computational mapping, null model, falsification test, and evidence class.
